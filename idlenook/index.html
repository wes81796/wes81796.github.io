The reason it exists

Good ideas shouldn’t
disappear into a chat.

While working with AI, I kept running into the same problem: useful ideas, decisions, and project updates were scattered across conversations. Keeping a separate record was becoming a project of its own.

Idlenook is my attempt to give that work a home. Not just a list of tasks, but a place to see how the pieces fit—and leave an unfinished thought without losing it.

Inside the workspace

Think in pieces.
See the whole.

Arrange the canvas around the way your mind works.

01 / Make space

Move ideas around.

Drag and resize cards. Connect a question to an experiment, or a small detail to the bigger idea behind it.

02 / Make it yours

Write where you think.

Edit directly on a card. Choose its color, font, and formatting without leaving the canvas.

03 / Keep things close

Bring the context along.

Drop an image to review and add it as a tile. Keep TXT, Markdown, and CSV documents in the workspace’s References.

For the work you do with AI

A shared map.
A clearer next step.

Give an authorized agent a place to record progress, decisions, and what remains—not another conversation you have to summarize by hand.

Personal stays separate.

Your personal library is not part of an agent workspace’s access. Choose deliberately what belongs on the shared board.

Access is explicit.

Agent workspaces support scoped writer and reviewer access, revision history, and revocation. Workspace images and references are shared with authorized agents.

You keep the layout.

Agent content updates preserve your card positions and sizes. The map can evolve without rearranging the space you made.

Local-first does not mean information can never leave your device. An external AI service receives what you share with it; revoking access cannot erase copies it already received.

Assistance, with a pause button

A second perspective.
Still your call.

Connect a model through Ollama to clarify a thought, challenge an assumption, or explore a next step. Review the selected context before sending it, then decide which suggestions to keep.

Model assistance is optional. This integration is still being refined, and its reliability depends on the model and setup. A locally running Ollama service may also use cloud-backed models.

Development snapshot · September 2026

The Windows desktop prototype is working and being prepared for a small round of friend testing. These are screenshots of the real application, using fictional demo content.

Next comes feedback on everyday use: onboarding, canvas clarity, model interactions, and the rough edges that only show up when someone else tries it.

There is no public download yet. Cross-platform releases, production security assurance, and real-time team collaboration are not completed features.

Behind the project

Made through
conversation and use.

I’m developing Idlenook collaboratively with Codex. I guide the product direction, test it in my own workflows, and bring back the “I wish it could…” moments that shape the next iteration. Codex contributes implementation, design exploration, and automated checks.

The aim is a useful, welcoming tool—not a claim that AI-generated code needs no review. Usability, reliability, and security validation remain ongoing.
