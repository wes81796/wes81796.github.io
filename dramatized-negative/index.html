The Dramatized Negative

I tested how local language models describe positive and negative experiences. Six instruct models gave negative human-conflict stories higher scene scores. The explanations I tested did not meet their pre-registered criteria.

Study frozen August 28, 2026 · Reporting clarified September 22, 2026

FindingsMethodsResultsPrediction outcomesLimitationsData & code

What the study found

  1. A consistent pattern in the tested human-conflict prompts. Negative stories had higher scene scores in all six instruct families: reported Cohen’s d values range from +0.51 to +1.07. This is a result for these models, prompts and judges, not a universal claim about language models.
  2. The explanations did not pass their tests. Neither initial framing prediction met its criterion. The later scoped framing and sensory-instruction predictions also failed. These outcomes leave the cause unresolved; they do not establish an alternative mechanism.
  3. The base comparison is limited. Only Llama and Mistral base models were tested. Their scene-gap estimates were smaller than their instruct counterparts in the human domain, but their intervals do not establish zero effect. Different prompting and filtering protocols prevent isolating post-training as the cause.

Read the uncertainty with the results. Each domain used three fixed prompt variants, with repeated generations within each. The original confidence intervals treat generations as independent and do not capture uncertainty over new phrasings. The judges were Claude-family model instances; agreement between them cannot rule out shared judging bias.

Methods and scope

Within each prompt variant, positive and negative prompts differed in the valence wording. Generations used temperature 0.8. The original comparison covered AI-user interactions and human-human interactions; later stages added exploratory domains, framing tests and an equal sensory-instruction test.

Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B and Qwen 3 8B initially; Gemma 3 12B, GLM-4 9B and Granite 3.3 8B in the final new-family test.

What the scores mean

Scene measures summary versus dramatized action: the reported anchors are 1 for pure summary and 7 for moment-by-moment action or quoted dialogue. Vividness and specificity are separate 1–7 judge dimensions; they are not interchangeable with scene. The archived score files name these dimensions, but this report does not establish the complete original rating instructions for them. Later Study 2 rubrics must not be substituted for the Study 1 instrument.

Valence gap means Cohen’s d, negative minus positive, using a pooled standard deviation. A positive scene gap means negative texts received higher scene scores. Relative attenuation means a smaller AI-domain gap than the human-domain gap. Inversion means a negative gap within one domain. The scoped P3 test specifically used service gap ≤ 0; that is a narrower condition than relative attenuation.

Scene means use the 1–7 scale. Gap estimates compare negative with positive within each model and domain. The Mistral AI-user scene estimate is −0.32, but its interval [−0.68, +0.04] spans zero. A point-estimate reversal should not be described as a precisely established negative effect.

All three initial instruct models have a smaller AI-user scene gap than human scene gap. This is a descriptive comparison; separate interval overlap is not a direct test of the difference between domain effects. This report does not provide uncertainty intervals for those differences.

The measures diverge. For example, Mistral’s human-domain scene gap is +0.56 while its vividness gap is +0.07. It would be misleading to restate every scene-score finding as “more vivid.”

What the comparison cannot isolate

The two tested base models had smaller estimated human-domain scene gaps than their instruct counterparts. However, the contrast bundles model training with prompt protocol, chat template, filtering, output length and lineage differences. It cannot establish that post-training caused the asymmetry, that pretraining contributed nothing, or that preference optimization is the mechanism.

The study’s original AI-user hypothesis was not supported as proposed: the initial instruct models’ AI-user scene gaps were smaller than their human-domain gaps. Descriptions such as “self-protection” were possible explanations considered after seeing this pattern, not measured model intentions.

Gemma, GLM and Granite were added for the character-framing test. All met the inclusion criterion of a positive human-domain scene estimate. Only Granite met P3’s service-gap ≤ 0 scope condition. All six instruct families had positive estimated human-conflict scene gaps. AI service estimates and shifts differed by model and prompt condition. These observations do not establish a universal “damping” behavior or locate its cause in a particular training pipeline.

The meal sensory-instruction test

Both valences were instructed to include exactly three specific sensory details. The reported meal vividness gaps were Qwen −1.25, Llama −0.03 and Mistral −2.85. Their arithmetic mean was −1.38, exceeding the registered magnitude threshold of 0.65; Mistral also exceeded its 1.58 threshold. P5a and P5b therefore failed.

Equal instructions are not verified equal content. The Study 1 report does not establish a compliance audit showing that the outputs actually contained equal sensory detail. Failure of these predictions does not rule out every genre or framing explanation, and it does not prove active suppression of negative content.

Sensitivity to wording

The reported Gemma service scene gaps across three variants were +3.02, +1.75 and −0.73 (spread 3.76). Meal-instruction vividness gaps were Mistral −2.70, −3.23 and −2.98; Qwen −1.81, −0.93 and −1.14; Llama −0.17, −0.09 and +0.23. These summaries show substantial prompt dependence and differences between models. Consistent signs across three selected phrasings do not establish generality to new phrasings.

The study stopped after these verdicts. The findings remain descriptive, with their causes unresolved.

Limitations that matter to the conclusions

  • Three fixed phrasings per domain. Repeated generations are nested within variants. The original interval calculation treats generations as independent and does not estimate generalization to new prompts. The large variant differences in the final round make this a material limitation.
  • Judge agreement is not construct validation. The original report gives Pearson r = 0.88–0.91 and 99.5% of rating pairs within one point for a 440-text repeat sample. Both passes used Claude-family judging instances, not independent human raters. They can share a bias, including conflating positive sensory language with vividness. The exact original vividness/specificity instructions need clearer archival documentation.
  • Base/instruct confounding. Only two base comparators were tested, with different prompting, templates, filtering and truncation. Confidence intervals spanning zero do not prove an absent base effect. The data do not isolate post-training.
  • Descriptive contrasts and limited uncertainty reporting. The displayed intervals are for individual gaps, not direct domain or framing contrasts. Final-family estimates lack reported intervals. Neither interval overlap nor a rounded zero is a substitute for a contrast analysis.
  • Missing and filtered observations. Initial Mistral-base AI scoring has 52 observations per valence; Llama-base AI has 60 positive and 59 negative. Gemma positive service has one missing final-round rating. Contamination filtering changes the population of retained base outputs; balance in loss counts would not by itself remove selection bias.
  • Limited model coverage. Six instruct families and two base models, approximately 7–12B parameters, with quantization differences. The study does not establish behavior at other scales, precisions or deployments.
  • Instruction compliance and mechanisms. Sensory detail was requested equally, not demonstrated to be equal. Failed operational predictions constrain those predictions; they do not eliminate every possible form of a proposed mechanism.
  • Selected examples and multiple stages. Excerpts are illustrative. Exploratory probes and post-hoc explanations are separated from prespecified directional tests. The frozen numbers are retained; this editorial correction is not an independent replication.
